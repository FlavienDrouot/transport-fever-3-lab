Model & inspiration

THE MODEL

Three phases, one simple hypothesis.

Maximum tractive effort at the start, then maximum power, followed by a steady top speed.

F(v) = min(Fmax, P / v)

Starting from rest on level track, with no resistance, braking or stops. A hypothesis to compare with observations, rather than a confirmed description of the game’s physics.

THE INSPIRATION

From a video race to a theoretical model.

This site was inspired by the video I Raced Every Multiple Unit in Transport Fever 3! Which one is the best?, by Flowengineer. Timings recorded from this video helped compare the theoretical model with how the trains behave in the game.

Mass, tractive effort, power and top speed are used to calculate the curves and explore the race.

How to read crossover charts

Phase focus expands the leadership phases and gives the final phase only 30% of the space of an earlier phase. Both axes stay continuous, with real distances and times at the ticks; their scale changes at the crossover boundaries. Curves are clipped to the displayed window. The window ends shortly after the last leader change; crossings between slower trains do not extend it.

With multiple leadership phases, orange grid lines and double marks show changes in a Phase focus scale. They can introduce bends in curves, independently of train acceleration. A single phase has no scale changes. Choose Linear on both axes for uniform scales.

What are we comparing?

Maintenance cost per passenger journey — lower is better.

How is this calculated?

For the selected distance and occupancy, we calculate each train’s A–B–A passenger throughput, then divide its maintenance cost by the number of passenger journeys it can provide in one game year. Lower is better. Values are absolute: selecting another train does not change them.

Maintenance per journey = maintenance cost ÷ (journeys per second × 1,460)

The calendar runs at four simulation seconds per day, with 365 days per year: 1,460 simulation seconds per game year. A value of $900 means $900 of maintenance per completed passenger journey. An outward journey and its return count separately. This is maintenance cost only, not a ticket price or total operating cost. The calculation assumes continuous service and this calendar speed.

Why exclude ticket revenue?

We assume the fare depends on the distance between stations and is the same for every train, consistent with our measurements on one route. For the same station pair, revenue is therefore fare × passenger throughput. If we equalize throughput, revenue is identical: maintenance is what differentiates the trains. Scaling fleets proportionally gives the ratio shown here.

This comparison excludes purchase costs, whole-train fleet rounding, congestion and the effect of journey duration on demand. It does not compare the total profit of one individual train. Revenue assumptions remain empirical rather than a complete description of the game’s pricing.

Assumptions & limitations

One train, equal occupancy in both directions, unlimited demand. At each terminal, all passengers leave before the next group boards. Each leg starts from rest; constant braking is included. Platform penalties, traffic and variable waits for late passengers are excluded.

Distance is shared with the race chart. An hour means 3,600 simulation seconds.

Experimental calibration: 1 passenger/s per multiplier unit, in both directions. Total rate = base rate × multiplier per car × number of cars. Braking defaults to 2.5 m/s²; fixed delays to 6 seconds per terminal (2 before unloading, 2 before loading, 2 before departure). All cars are assumed to contribute equally. Empty mass is used; passenger load showed no visible acceleration effect.

This model assumes enough passengers to sustain the chosen occupancy, regardless of journey time. Very long journeys can reduce demand in the game. The Handcar’s very low maintenance can therefore make it the theoretical winner here, while its slow service may attract too few passengers on long routes. Demand is not modelled, so this ranking is not a complete recommendation for a real line. Deselect the Handcar to compare the other trains.

Understanding the results

A stop includes unloading, loading and fixed delays. Travel includes braking to rest. Both directions count as passenger journeys; occupancy can represent an average. Maintenance per journey divides the game’s maintenance cost by journeys provided in 1,460 simulation seconds. Lower values need less maintenance for equal throughput. It excludes fares and purchase costs.

Reading the graphs

The distance axis ends at your selected route distance, shown by the vertical marker. Hover a curve to identify its train. Log scale crops values below the displayed minimum.

Phase focus expands leadership phases and compresses the final phase. Marks on the distance axis show scale changes. The displayed window runs from 100 m to your selected route; no claim is made beyond that distance. A single leadership phase keeps a uniform scale.

All crossovers gives equal space between every ranking change, including changes among non-leading trains. Crossings before 100 m are omitted. The chart ends at the selected route distance.
