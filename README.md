# TF3 Lab

**Compare vehicles in Transport Fever 3, build trains and routes, and experiment with travel time and service costs.**

[Explore the site](https://flaviendrouot.github.io/transport-fever-3-lab/)

An open-source, independent community project for railway and road comparisons. Explore modelled acceleration, arrival times and changes in ranking using captured specifications and documented experiments.

## Start exploring

The site has three spaces: **Compare**, **Design** and **Data & models**. Compare keeps **Race / Service** in fixed tabs. The sidebar’s **Rail / Road** and **Passengers / Freight** selectors apply to both views; switching domain keeps the transport category. Each domain retains its own vehicle selection, year and service targets. The route is shared.

1. Try **Avelia vs TGV** or **Avelia vs Fuxing** for a passenger-train duel on the current route, or choose vehicles in **Route & vehicles**. Search, sort and filter by introduction year without losing hidden selections.
2. **Race** shows acceleration, distance/time, arrival rankings and crossovers. On a constant-grade/limit route, the speed chart focuses on acceleration independently of route length. On a changing route, it follows the route to B. Freight railway comparisons use your compositions; an empty catalogue invites you to build one.
3. **Service** compares Running costs per transported capacity on A–B–A. Set **Utilization**, then optionally **Targets & constraints** to size a whole fleet for Rate or Frequency. With Freight selected, open **Freight handling & facilities** for Loaded return and separate Terminal/Warehouse bonuses at A and B. Rail also offers coupling of identical multiple units and a maximum train length. These targets do not change Race or the single-train composition curves. **Show calculation details** reveals cycle timing and handling.
4. **Simple / Custom route** switches between quick distance, gradient and speed settings and the route designed from segments. Each keeps its settings during the session. Opening the route editor leaves the active route unchanged; editing or **Use this route** activates the custom route. Route presets are 100/160/350 km/h for Rail and 50/80/120 for Road; the numeric limit accepts 10–350 km/h. Rail and Road retain independent speed limits on the same route (initially 350 and 120 km/h). The optimizer and custom segment editor expose both limits. Frequency inputs use minutes + seconds; the optimizer can also bound each leg’s travel time and explains identified constraints when no solution fits.
5. **Design → Composition** assembles railway or tram vehicles and quantities, then shows a steady-running reference and transient curves. These describe one train at uniform gradient, limit and utilization, independently of route segments and service targets. **Route & service** uses the active shared route and offers the same Simple/Custom switch. Open a valid draft in Compare to experiment without saving; **Save composition** keeps it in browser storage after reload. Tram compositions appear in Road with **Include trams** enabled.
6. **Design → Route** edits one to 24 segments, with a symbolic elevation sketch, direct segment selection and insert/move/remove actions. Grades span −20% to +20%, segment lengths start at 10 m, and the total route is limited to 100 km. The editor always offers 30/50/60/80/100/120/160/350 km/h presets for all vehicles. Choose equal segment widths or linear distance; elevation is exaggerated.
7. **Data & models → Catalogue / Models / Checks** groups downloadable specifications, calculation methods, inspiration and experimental evidence. Short help remains beside the controls and charts.

**Design → Optimizer** is an experimental service search. Choose the shared Simple/Custom route, transport category and freight specialization, then choose **Rail + Road / Rail only / Road only**. Set the required **Rate**, optional minimum and maximum **Frequency** intervals between vehicles (for example 3–5 minutes), and **Utilization** ceiling. Frequency intervals use a single framed minutes:seconds control with a fixed colon; independent optional **Max leg time A→B** and **Max leg time B→A** limits constrain each direction, excluding stop handling. Empty results explain identifiable limiting constraints. Both Frequency interval limits are disabled by default; enabling the minimum starts at 0 min 30 s. It minimizes whole-fleet annual **Running costs**, grouping Rail proposals by locomotive or multiple-unit model. Every feasible wagon model retains its three cheapest formation variants; multiple units retain their three cheapest repeated-trainset variants. A single detail card above the list shows the selected configuration, defaulting to the cheapest service. An always-visible comparison table initially shows five locomotive/MU rows, with Show more revealing five more at a time. Locomotive/MU and wagon rows remain expandable; Select updates the card, while Open composition applies that selection. Groups and wagon choices are ordered by their cheapest service. Road uses the same selected-card and grouped-table layout, retaining every feasible bus/truck/tram model and its three cheapest formation variants. Mixed light-rail compositions have their own group. Five model rows are shown initially, with Show more revealing the next five. The **Route & infrastructure** column groups route settings, **Tracks & roads**, and optional **Stops & infrastructure**. Route upkeep is excluded by default; enable Include route Running costs to choose tiers per segment within the game year, with imposed 2/4 Road lanes. City and custom curve speed limits cannot be raised. Rail tiers unlock at 100 km/h initially, 160 in 1930, and 350 in 1980; Road has only 40/60 km/h before 1940. Selected tiers and route upkeep appear in proposals and their speed limits transfer when opened. Stops & infrastructure is expanded by default. Use **Add existing reusable infrastructure**, then choose the stop A/B, Rail/Road only when both are searched, and the reusable part with segmented toggles: **Station only** or **Station + platform**. A station alone requires a new platform; an available platform is reused. Stops/domains without declarations include new terminals automatically, within **New infrastructure · limits**. Each entry has its relevant length, extension and specialization permissions. Rail platform maxima default to 840 m and can be reduced; when infrastructure is enabled, they replace the separate train-length input. Rail search retains a disclosed game platform maximum of 840 m. Multiple available entries at one stop are alternatives; the search cannot fall back to an undeclared new station there. Entries can be removed; those outside the selected domain are retained without entering its search. Existing upkeep is excluded; only new installations, extensions and specialization upgrades are counted. The optimizer sizes Rail platforms in 40 m sections and chooses allowed specialization and platform-track count along with the vehicles. One train occupies each track, and two tracks can share one platform. Declare reusable track/platform counts and permissions; new-terminal bounds initially permit two platform tracks. Rail terminal capacity includes flat entry, handling and accelerated departure over half the train length plus 100 m of switches. Configurations requiring waiting are excluded; shared switches may reduce capacity in practice. For freight, choose the actual endpoint building first: Industry, Warehouse or Industrial building. Industry Road terminals are automatically specialized and free; no separate terminal declaration is needed. Each serves one vehicle at a time, with two parallel terminals by default and a 2 / 3 toggle in the endpoint card. Warehouses remain fixed route inputs; existing capacity and specialization are retained, expansion requires permission and a maximum capacity, and existing upkeep is excluded. Each delivery load must fit both warehouses. Results show the upkeep added by the proposed infrastructure changes. Road platforms start at 20 m; extensions add 10 m, priced per 10 m with no parallel-lane surcharge. Set maximum length and parallel-platform count at each stop (new-terminal UI defaults: 40 m and two platforms). Reusable declarations specify existing length/count, and extension or parallel additions require permission. The search compares long, parallel and mixed-length layouts. Serial places depend on vehicle length, with at least one place per platform. Regular-arrival throughput and mean blocking wait are estimated without vehicle simulation; cycles include waiting and 5 s maneuvering per visit. Proposals show geometry, maximum vehicles/min and estimated mean wait. Traffic and queue propagation between stops are outside the estimate. A bus stop holds one vehicle and costs $30,000/year; it can serve passengers or unload freight at industrial destination B without a loaded return. Allow bus stop permits comparison with a new station; an existing bus stop can be declared for reuse. Purchase prices and amortization are outside the ranking. **Year & search bounds** limits purchase availability using collected source retirement dates, train length, component quantities and fleet size. **Ignore retirements** is checked by default; uncheck it to respect source retirement dates. Introduction years still apply. Unselected domains are not searched. Rail searches identical complete trainsets repeated up to the maximum train length, with optional quantities of one wagon model, or one locomotive model with one wagon model. The locomotive count bound applies only to locomotive + wagon formations; it does not cap repeated trainsets. MU + same-category railway wagon combinations are searched when identical-only coupling is disabled; the MU quantity is bounded by terminal/train length rather than the locomotive count. Rail also searches the nine confirmed Rail-compatible trams as whole units, with homogeneous and mixed same-category compositions. They never mix with classical railway stock. Under **Tracks & roads**, enable **Include trams in Road search** to add coupled complete trams and tram locomotive or ordinary powered tram + wagon formations to buses/trucks. Set Road tracks / Dedicated tracks / Auto for each segment. Road tram tracks have no extra upkeep and cannot use highway tiers 100/120; dedicated tracks cost $20,000/km/year for both directions and are limited to 100 km/h. City and custom curve caps remain constraints. Route upkeep stays optional and off by default. Saved recipes and heterogeneous railway engine/wagon formations remain outside the search. All freight means general-purpose vehicles. Open a Rail proposal as an unsaved composition, or open Road Service with its vehicle/unsaved tram composition and targets selected. Road tram proposals also offer Edit composition. Changing settings invalidates the proposals; a running search can be cancelled. With **Include route Running costs** enabled (off by default), **Rail tracks** offers Auto / 2 tracks per segment: Auto chooses one track for one train, otherwise two, one per direction; route upkeep is charged per track. Passing loops and signal capacity are outside this model. A live **Your constraints** sidebar recaps the route, service goals, active search bounds and infrastructure permissions. It stays on the right on desktop and becomes a compact disclosure above the inputs on mobile.

Hover or focus a curve, label or vehicle to identify it across the page. Linear/log axes and **Phase focus** make different parts of the comparisons easier to inspect. Invalid numeric settings retain the last valid calculation and show a message. Download CSV or SVG from the speed and distance/time sections. The theme and saved compositions persist locally; Simple/Custom routes last only for the current session. There is no sign-in. Optimizer settings and proposals are temporary; only saving a composition writes it to browser storage.

## What the comparisons mean

Motion uses empty mass, traction, power and maximum speed. Displayed traction stays in newtons; the acceleration model applies an effective ×2 multiplier. Each 0.2-second step updates speed and advances using the mean of initial and final speed:

```text
a(v) = min(2 × Fmax, P / v) / m − 0.02 − 0.38 × g × sin(atan(grade / 100))
```

Flat rail acceleration is calibrated against synchronized Avelia/TGV measurements. MAN road tests and a TGV Duplex run on a displayed 5% slope validate the shared gravity factor of 0.38. Rail braking uses the calibrated 2.5 m/s² rate, anticipating lower segment limits; services stop at A and B, while Race ends at B without a terminal stop. Road acceleration uses the same structure, but braking is omitted: the available MAN/FAW stopping tests do not establish a common constant deceleration. Road lower limits apply instantly, and each service leg restarts without adding stopping time or distance. Tram motion needs independent calibration.

“Best” in Race means fastest for the distance. Service means lowest Running costs per transported capacity, including travel, handling, terminal pauses and optional whole-fleet rounding. Passenger target Rate is per direction; freight target Rate counts loaded deliveries. Road passenger tables report total Rate across both directions. Facilities double handling at each selected stop, with terminal and warehouse bonuses stacking to ×4. Capacity denotes transported load in cost denominators. Costs use four simulation seconds per day and 365 days per game year, at normal difficulty.

Purchase, traffic, full queue simulation, ticket revenue and demand generated by travel time are excluded; Optimizer estimates Road terminal waiting under regular arrivals. Compare and Composition exclude infrastructure costs; Optimizer can include the infrastructure upkeep attributed to the project, but excludes construction and amortization. Assuming sufficient demand can favour slow, low-maintenance vehicles even when their real occupancy would be unrealistic. Compare filters by introduction year; Optimizer also uses source retirement dates, without applying active save/mod overrides. Distance sweeps proportionally scale custom segment lengths; crossover searches are sampled and may miss narrow fleet boundaries. [Model and data notes](docs/model.md) describe the evidence and conditions for revisiting these assumptions.

## Run locally

Requires Node.js 22+ and Python 3. No npm dependencies need installing.

```sh
git clone https://github.com/FlavienDrouot/transport-fever-3-lab.git
cd transport-fever-3-lab
npm test
npm run dev
```

Open http://127.0.0.1:4173. `npm run build` creates a static site in `dist/`. See [development and deployment](docs/development.md) for remote previews, repository structure and GitHub Pages.

The Composition displays original vehicle miniatures in its catalogue and composition cards. Complete formation previews reuse their component images in order. Game artwork is separate from the MIT-licensed code; see [the thumbnail notice](assets/vehicle-thumbnails/NOTICE.md).

Tram locomotive source cards are stored separately in [`data/tram-locomotives.json`](data/tram-locomotives.json) as component choices in the Composition editor. The five records retain their captured costs and mechanical properties. Six passenger tram wagons are stored in [`data/tram-passenger-wagons.json`](data/tram-passenger-wagons.json), with capacity, handling and source values. Fifteen freight tram wagons are stored in [`data/tram-freight-wagons.json`](data/tram-freight-wagons.json), grouped by freight specialization. Open **Design → Composition**, choose **Tram**, then combine these components with powered passenger/freight trams. Save named compositions to compare them in **Road** with **Include trams** enabled. They can be edited, reordered or removed; definitions are retained in browser storage and their totals are recalculated from the current catalogue after reload. Tram coupling is supported. Rail-compatible tram units are available in both carrier catalogues and may only couple with other Rail-compatible trams of the same category; they cannot mix with railway stock or other tram components. Individual wagons/locomotives are not ranked as standalone services.

The **Data & models → Catalogue** inventory also exposes collected installation metadata, including locomotives, wagons and future vehicles. All road and tram entries outside campaign content, plus the captured railway roster, are matched to the calculator and configurator datasets. Remaining internal railway components and other transport categories stay outside the calculators until their names and usable values have been resolved. See [the metadata import workflow](docs/development.md#import-a-collected-metadata-catalogue).

Thirty purchasable railway locomotives are stored in [`data/rail-locomotives.json`](data/rail-locomotives.json), combining condensed captures with uniquely matched extracted model values. EMD F-Unit and Chinese Class SS4G retain their complete two-element formation. All thirty are named in the Data & models catalogue; locomotives alone are not ranked as passenger services. All seventeen multiple units use extracted aggregate handling, including zero-loading-speed power cars and single-model articulated railcars.

The complete captured railway wagon roster contains 16 passenger carriages in [`data/rail-passenger-wagons.json`](data/rail-passenger-wagons.json) and 27 freight wagons in [`data/rail-freight-wagons.json`](data/rail-freight-wagons.json). Condensed captures supply identity, capacity, handling and purchase costs; matched extracted metadata supplies mass, model extent and maintenance. The British Mark 3 retains two equivalent internal resource references as one configurable choice. Together with the 30 locomotives and 17 multiple units, these form 90 railway choices, plus nine Rail-compatible trams, in **Design → Composition → Rail**. Passenger compositions join Race and Service; freight compositions join Race and the Freight mode in Service. Route settings and the vehicle selection share the right panel in the Compare views; Data & models keeps the full-width inventory.

Road tram compositions have an independent, adjustable **Maximum Road tram length** in Optimizer’s **Year & search bounds**, default 80 m. This practical limit includes dedicated tram tracks and applies to complete, mixed and powered/wagon Road tram formations; Rail-compatible trams searched with Rail retain the railway platform/train limit.

Optimizer’s **Year & search bounds** includes **Couple MUs only with identical units**, checked by default (`identicalMUsOnly:true`). It applies to classical railway trainsets and powered trams in both domains: identical repetitions remain, while added wagons and heterogeneous light-rail tram formations are skipped before service evaluation. Unchecking restores those searches. Locomotive + wagon formations are unaffected. Different classical railway MU models are not combined, even with this option disabled. The setting changes optimizer search only; it does not restrict the Composition builder.
